If intelligence becomes as cheap and available as water and electricity, where does science go?

倘若智能如水电一样便宜、易得，科学将何去何从？

My work treats that question as concrete. Open quantum dynamics is a resource for computation and a target for learning: I design quantum algorithms in which dissipation does useful work, solving differential equations, estimating phases, and discovering rare events, and I prove what a learner with limited access to a quantum system can recover, and at what cost.

Alongside this, I study when quantum linear algebra speeds up the models machine learning actually uses, and I am building AI tools that make scientific reasoning verifiable, so that cheap intelligence can be trusted.

Research

Four directions share one point of view: what a computational model can do is decided by what it actually provides, the dynamics it can run, the access it has to data, and the form of its output. The results worth having are the ones whose cost can be checked against a lower bound.

The research program

  1. Open-system dynamics as a computational resource

    Lindbladian evolution can encode linear differential equations with near-optimal query complexity, and fast-forwardable Lindbladians turn out to imply Heisenberg-limited phase estimation. The same viewpoint gives a quantum algorithm that discovers and samples rare events without knowing them in advance.

    PRL 2025QIP 2026arXiv 2026

  2. Learning quantum systems from limited access

    Protocols and matching lower bounds for learning local Lindbladians from finite-time probes, for learning states with graphical-model structure, and for the online setting, where pure states turn out to be as hard to learn as mixed ones.

    arXiv 2026PRR 2026NeurIPS 2025

  3. Quantum linear algebra for machine learning

    Quantum linear-algebra routines for the operations inside Transformers and multilayer networks, with the accounting done honestly: which input models, which output representations, and which regimes still show a speedup.

    arXiv 2024ICLR 2026PRR 2024

  4. Verifiable AI for research

    A prototype that reconstructs the evidence trail behind a scientific claim and tests reasoning against proofs or benchmarks, starting with quantum information.
